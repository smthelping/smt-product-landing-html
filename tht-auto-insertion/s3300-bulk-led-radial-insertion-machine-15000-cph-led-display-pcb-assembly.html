Why buyers call us

What hand insertion and taped LEDs cost you

LED boards carry hundreds of identical parts. That is exactly where people get tired and taped parts get expensive.

01

You pay extra for tape

Taped LEDs carry a taping premium on every piece. On a display line that runs hundreds of thousands of LEDs a day, the tape can cost more than the labour.

02

Hands set the ceiling

A skilled operator typically inserts 1,500–2,000 LEDs an hour. More output means more people, more benches and more training.

03

Reversed LEDs show up late

A reversed LED is invisible until the board lights up after soldering. Then it is rework, not a quick fix.

04

Dead LEDs reach the wave

A dead or weak LED that gets soldered costs a desoldering job and a second test pass.

05

Colours get mixed

Red, green and blue LEDs look alike before they light up. A mixed bin turns into a whole batch of rework.

06

Uneven height, loose leads

LEDs that tilt or float before wave soldering spoil the look of a display module and slow down final assembly.

Feature · Advantage · Benefit

Six things the S3300 does for your LED line

Three bulk feeders

Feature

3 bowl feeder sets, one per colour, fed with loose Ø3 / Ø5 mm LEDs.

Advantage

Each colour has its own bowl, track and sensors, so feeding is independent and colours stay apart.

Benefit

Buy LEDs in bags instead of tape and run red, green and blue on one board without a changeover.

Polarity & good/bad check

Feature

Intelligent polarity and good/bad detection on every feeder.

Advantage

Reversed LEDs are turned 180° and re-checked; bad LEDs are rejected before they reach the head.

Benefit

Fewer reversed and dead LEDs reach the solder wave, so less rework at final test.

15,000 points / hour

Feature

Actual insertion speed of 15,000 points per hour on a full servo base drive with a bus-controlled AC servo system.

Advantage

Speed comes from servo motion, not from extra operators.

Benefit

One machine covers volume that would otherwise need a bench of hand inserters (see the example scenario below).

Any-angle insertion & clinch

Feature

Insertion at any angle; leads bent 1.2 / 1.5 / 1.8 mm long at 10–35° outward.

Advantage

Each LED is locked to the board before it leaves the machine.

Benefit

LEDs stay upright and even in height through wave soldering, which matters on visible display modules.

Machine vision

Feature

Intelligent machine vision system for hole position correction.

Advantage

Board marks are read and every hole position is corrected for board tolerance.

Benefit

Dense display boards are inserted accurately without manual alignment.

In-line, Excel programming

Feature

Automatic board transfer left-to-right or right-to-left; programs imported by USB as Excel files; online programming.

Advantage

The machine fits the flow direction of your existing line and takes coordinates you already have.

Benefit

New LED layouts go from file to first board quickly, with no special programming skills.

Operation

Changeover in six steps

  1. Load the new program by USB (Excel file).
  2. Teach or confirm the MARK points with the vision camera.
  3. Assign a colour bowl to each LED position.
  4. Fill the bowls with loose LEDs.
  5. Run a first board and check polarity and results.
  6. Start production; board transfer runs automatically.

 

Simple upkeep

Every shiftClean bowls and tracks, empty the reject bin, check air pressure (0.6 MPa).
WeeklyClean the detection sensors and camera window; check cutter blades and clinch parts.
MonthlyLubricate screws and guides, check belts and chain, back up programs.
Every 6 monthsCalibrate axes and vision; check servo cables and air filters.

FAQ

Questions buyers ask about the S3300

Do I need taped LEDs?

No. The S3300 takes bulk (loose) LEDs with Ø3 or Ø5 mm bodies and a 2.5 mm lead pitch straight into its three bowl feeders.

How fast is the S3300?

The spec sheet gives an actual insertion speed of 15,000 points per hour. Real output depends on hole layout, board transfer and colour changes; send your board file for a cycle-time estimate.

How does it handle LED polarity?

Each of the three feeders has intelligent polarity and good/bad detection. A reversed LED is turned 180° and checked again; an LED that fails is rejected before insertion.

Can it run red, green and blue on one board?

Yes. Each of the three feeder sets holds one colour, and the program assigns a colour to every LED position.

What board sizes fit?

Boards from 50 × 50 mm up to 300 × 400 mm can be loaded. The insertion area is 300 × 330 mm, or 300 × 400 mm on the dual-camera version. Board thickness 0.79–2.36 mm.

What clinch settings are available?

Bend length 1.2, 1.5 or 1.8 mm (adjustable) and bend angle 10–35°, bent outward.

Can it insert at any angle?

Yes. The spec sheet lists the insertion direction as any angle.

How do we program a new board?

Import the insertion coordinates by USB as an Excel file (online programming is supported), confirm the MARK points with the vision camera and assign a colour to each position.

What utilities does it need?

Single-phase 220 V, 50/60 Hz, 2.0 kW; compressed air 0.6 MPa at 0.38 m³/min; 5–30 °C ambient. The main unit weighs approx. 1,200 kg.
