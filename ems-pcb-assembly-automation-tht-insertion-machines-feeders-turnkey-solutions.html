Field Note 01 · Manual-to-Auto Economics

The Hidden Cost of Hand-Placing Through-Hole Components: Why Your SMT Line Outruns Your THT Bench

The 3-second story every EMS plant manager recognizes — and the three-gate check that tells you exactly when to automate.

The 3-Second Reality Check

Your pick-and-place machine places 50,000 components an hour. Twenty meters down the line, a row of operators hand-inserts capacitors and connectors at a few hundred per hour each — and your output ledger pretends nothing is wrong. In most high-mix EMS plants, the SMT side is not the constraint. The through-hole bench is.

Where the Money Actually Leaks

Manual THT insertion quietly bills you four times: rework from bent leads and reversed polarity; scrap from parts pressed into misaligned holes; training cost as labor churns through a 30–60% annual rotation; and the audit risk of an untraceable process when an automotive or medical customer walks the floor. A single escaped wrong-polarity electrolytic can cost more than a month of an operator’s wage — in customer penalties alone.

The Three-Gate Automation Check

Before buying any inserter, run your product through three gates. Gate 1 — Product audit: list every THT part by package type (radial, axial, terminal, odd-form) and pitch. Gate 2 — Process audit: measure your true cost-per-point using the CPI formula (depreciation + spares + labor + maintenance ÷ inserted points), not the marketing brochure’s CPH. Gate 3 — Volume gate: if a board family stays above roughly 500 boards a day for 6+ months, automation pays; below that, our smart feeders on your existing mounter often beat a dedicated inserter. Boards that pass all three gates are your automation candidates.

The Machines That Do It

For boards that pass the gates, the vehicle is the S3010A radial / S4000 axial pair plus the S-2000 jumper inserter: under 500 PPM insertion defects, vision-corrected placement, and — the part your CFO cares about — 100% compatibility with Universal-class wear parts at under $100 a month, with ROI typically inside 12 months at modest loading.

Keeping It Profitable Year After Year

Automation is not a one-and-done purchase. The loop that keeps it profitable: free 24-hour sampling before you commit; live video verification of your actual boards running on the actual machine; one-day spares delivery so a worn cutter never stops the line; and a platform that grows with you — start with one radial head, add axial and odd-form cells as volume climbs, all on the same Windows software your operators already learned.

Field Note 02 · Terminal & Odd-Form Strategy

One Inserter, Ten Bench Workers: The Real Math Behind Terminal and Odd-Form Automation

Terminals, eyelets, transformers and connectors are where THT automation usually dies. Here is the platform rule that keeps it alive.

The Power-Board Problem

Every power-supply, charger and appliance board carries the same crew: reel terminals, eyelets, header pins, tactile switches, relays, transformers. Mixed geometries, mixed packaging, mixed hole patterns. Most plants solve it with a bench of ten hand-loaders — and lose the same quality lottery every single shift.

Why Standard Inserters Fail Here

Radial and axial machines eat taped components happily — then you hand them a bulk FASTON terminal or a 25 mm transformer and everything stops. That is the real reason “automation doesn’t work for our products” spreads through EMS procurement teams: not because automation cannot place the part, but because nobody built a changeable feeding-and-gripping front end for it.

The 5-in-1 Platform Rule

The fix is architectural, not heroic: keep one rigid machine base (X/Y servo table, CCD vision, clinch anvil, Windows control) and treat the feeder stack and gripper head as swappable modules. Feed terminals from reels, eyelets from vibratory bowls, switches from tubes, odd parts from trays. Changeover becomes a feeder-and-gripper swap in under an hour instead of a new machine purchase.

The Working Horses

That rule is exactly how our S7020 series is built: one base, five personalities — S7020T reel/bulk terminals at 12,000 CPH with 0.55 s cycles, S7020E eyelets with rivet flowering, S7020P header pins, S7020S tactile switches, S7020F odd-form — and the S7900 extends the same philosophy to large connectors with four automatic clinch modes that prevent post-wave floating. Priced at roughly one third of Universal-class machines, one cell typically displaces a ten-worker bench.

The Compounding Loop

Because every variant shares one software and one parts library, each new product family costs you a recipe — not a machine. Spares stay under $100 a month, MES hooks (QR verification, miss-insertion detection with auto-patch, telemetry export) carry straight across the family, and your second cell installs in days because your operators already run the first.

Field Note 03 · Low-Capex Feeding Route

Automate THT Feeding Without Buying a New Inserter: The $6K Feeder That Unlocks Your Idle Mounter

Your SMT machine already has the precision. It is just starving. Nine feeding modes, one interface — and no new inserter required.

The Idle-Machine Paradox

Walk most EMS floors at 2 a.m. and you will find a $200K mounter waiting for a feeder change while a night-shift worker hand-inserts bulk connectors two benches away. The placement accuracy you need — ±0.05 mm at speed — already exists on your SMT line. What is missing is feeding: nobody taught your mounter to eat radial tape, tubes, bowls or bulk parts.

The Multi-Vendor Trap

The traditional fix is ugly: one vendor’s tube feeder, another’s bowl, a third’s label feeder — each with its own connector, its own 2–4 week integration, its own 3–5 day operator training, and a spare-parts directory nobody can maintain. Mid-volume plants end up with five feeding islands and a changeover process measured in shifts. That is why the automation budget dies in committee, every year.

One Supplier, Seven Feeding Modes

The framework that fixes it: standardize the interface, not the vendor list. Every feeder in our platform speaks the same 8-core Φ16 mm aviation plug, takes 24V or 220V, runs on 0.4–0.6 MPa shop air, and switches between IO mode and full self-control. That means a radial-tape feeder, an axial-tape feeder, a dual-rail tube feeder, a double-deck bowl, a bulk belt, a label feeder and a tray feeder all bolt to the same mounter slot — changeover in minutes, one training day for the whole family, and one spares list.

The $6K Escape Hatch

Here is the number that rewrites the business case: a dedicated THT inserter runs $50–70K. A smart feeder that gives the mounter you already own the same through-hole capability runs $5–8K — cycles of 0.8–3 seconds, ±0.2–1 mm precision, 5-million-cycle cutter life, proven on Panasonic, JUKI, YAMAHA, Mirae, FUJI and SIPLACE platforms. For mid-volume, high-mix boards, that is the entire decision.

From One Feeder to Smart Material Flow

The platform scales past the machine: SIS7000A / SIS4000A smart racks hold 1,400 / 400 reels and talk MQTT to your MES/ERP via EMQX — kitting drops to ~15 minutes, and every part is traceable from stores to placement. Start with one feeder on one product; grow into a fully logged, lights-out material flow without ever re-integrating a vendor.
