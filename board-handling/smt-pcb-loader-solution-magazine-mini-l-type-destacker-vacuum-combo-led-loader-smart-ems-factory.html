How it works

How a magazine loader feeds your line

Six steps from a full magazine to a board on the printer or mounter conveyor, the same on every SLD, SLF and S-MLD loader.

1

Load full magazines

An operator, an AGV or the upstream unloader places full magazines on the upper conveyor. Pneumatic clamps align each magazine top and bottom.

Standard 355 to 535 mm ESD magazines, 50 boards each.
2

Index to the next board

The elevator steps the magazine by the set pitch so the next board sits level with the conveyor.

Pitch 10, 20, 30 or 40 mm, or set to your magazine.
3

Push the board out

A regulated pneumatic or motor pusher, adjusted to the centre of the magazine, pushes the board onto the conveyor without stressing it.

About 6 s per board; pusher pressure regulated.
4

Hand over by SMEMA

The loader waits for the downstream machine's board-available signal and sends the board only when the next machine is ready.

SMEMA on every loader; 900 ± 20 mm transfer height.
5

Change the empty magazine

When a magazine is empty the elevator drops it to the lower conveyor and takes the next full one, while the line keeps running from the buffer.

Magazine change about 30 s.
6

Alarm before the line starves

Tower light and buzzer warn the operator before the last magazine runs out, so one person can look after several lines.

Sound and light alarms, fault codes on the panel.

Selection5 min read

Magazine, Stack or Pallet: Choosing a PCB Loader by How Boards Arrive

The right loader is decided before the board reaches the line. Start with how boards come out of the warehouse, not with a catalogue page.

The loader that did not fit the flow

A factory buys a fast magazine loader, then finds its bare boards arrive shrink-wrapped in stacks. Someone now spends the shift filling magazines by hand, which is the job the loader was meant to remove.

Why the supply form decides everything

A loader is the hand-over point between material handling and the process. Boards can arrive in magazines from an upstream line, as bare boards in stacks from the supplier, or as flexible circuits that must sit in a pallet. Each form needs a different mechanism: a pusher, a destacker or vacuum pick, or a robot.

Map the flow first

Write down, for every product, how boards arrive at the line, how many per lot, board size and thickness, and whether the line runs one side or both. Add the floor space you have at the line start. Those six facts choose the loader type before you compare any prices.

How our family covers each case

Magazines go to the SLD loaders for boards up to 530 x 460 mm, the SLF L-type where length is tight or the S-MLD250 mini loader for short lines. Bare stacks go to the SBU destacker with 200 boards per stack or the enclosed SVL vacuum loader. Double-sided work uses a combo loader with both sources. Long LED boards use the SLD350L, lead frames the SL600A and flexible boards the SFBL250.

Labour4 min read

The Hidden Cost of Feeding Boards by Hand

One person at the start of every line looks cheap. Multiply by lines, shifts and the boards they drop, and it rarely is.

The person nobody counts

Ask a production manager how many operators run a line and the person feeding boards at the front is often left out. Yet that position must be covered every minute the line runs, including breaks.

Why manual feeding costs more than a wage

Hand feeding sets an uneven rhythm, so the printer waits or boards pile up. Boards are touched, scratched or loaded the wrong way round. And when the feeder steps away, the whole line stops.

Count it properly

Count people who feed boards per line and per shift, including relief. Add the minutes the line waits for boards and the boards scrapped from handling. Compare that with one loader that only needs an operator to change a magazine every 50 boards.

What a loader changes

A magazine loader pushes about one board every 6 seconds and changes an empty magazine in about 30 seconds. Sound and light alarms warn before the last magazine empties, so one person can look after several lines. The mini loader's own sheet makes the same point: one worker can oversee multiple machines and lines.

Bare boards5 min read

Bare Boards: Destacker or Vacuum Loader?

Both feed bare boards from a stack. They differ in how they separate the next board, and that decides which boards they handle best.

Two boards come out at once

Bare boards from the supplier stick together: from vacuum packing, static or a burr on the edge. A loader that takes two boards jams the printer or ruins a stencil print.

Why separation is the hard part

A stack is not a magazine. There is no slot holding each board apart, so the loader must separate the bottom or top board by itself, without bending thin boards or marking the surface.

Pick by board and volume

Thicker, flat, rigid boards in high volume suit a destacker that pulls the bottom board out by belt. Thin, flexible or delicate boards, or boards that must be lifted clean off the top of the stack, suit a vacuum loader with suction pads you can position.

Our two answers

The SBU330 and SBU460 destackers hold 200 boards of 0.4 mm, with a thickness dial, slide-width adjustment and a pass-through mode. The SVL vacuum loaders hold 100 to 300 boards, lift the top board with adjustable suction cups inside an enclosed guard, and run the belt at 0.5 to 20 m/min. A long SVL version takes LED boards up to 1300 x 330 mm.

Test with your worst board

Send the thinnest and most warped board you run. A ten-minute trial shows whether the destacker separates it cleanly or whether you need vacuum pick.

Layout4 min read

When Floor Space Is Short: Mini and L-Type Loaders

A standard magazine loader adds up to 1.8 m to the front of a line. In a crowded hall that may be the space you do not have.

The line that would not fit

Many factories add a loader to an existing line and find the aisle, a pillar or the next line in the way. The choice then seems to be no automation or a new layout.

Why standard loaders take length

An in-line magazine loader holds full magazines on an upper conveyor and empty ones on a lower conveyor, both along the line direction. That buffer is what lets it run unattended, and it takes length.

Turn or shrink the loader

Two ways out: put the magazine lift beside the line at 90 degrees, or use a compact loader with a single magazine for short runs. Check the transfer height, direction and SMEMA as for any loader.

SLF L-type and S-MLD250 mini

The SLF L-type loaders place the lift to the side in a 1.15 to 1.55 m square, with two magazines of 50 boards, four pitch settings and adjustable push speed. The S-MLD250 mini loader is a single-magazine machine about 0.6 x 0.65 m on the product sheet, with a motor pusher, full-colour touch screen and a 50-board magazine.

Measure the real space

Measure from the first machine's board entry back to the nearest obstacle, and the width beside the line. Send both with your board size.

Double-sided4 min read

Double-Sided Boards with One Loader

The first side starts as a bare board, the second as a populated one. A combo loader feeds both without a second machine.

Two loaders, one line

Double-sided products are often run with a destacker for side one and a magazine loader for side two, swapped or placed in series. That costs floor space, money and changeover time.

Why the two sides differ

Side one boards are bare and arrive in stacks. Side two boards carry components and must travel in magazines so nothing touches them. One mechanism rarely handles both.

Combine the sources

Put a stack source and a magazine source in one machine and select the mode on the panel. Check component clearance for side two and the stack height for side one.

Our combo loaders

The SLD250-D and SLD390-D combine magazine and destacker: pitch 10 to 40 mm, component clearance 80 mm top and 20 mm bottom, 200 mm stack. The SLD250-V, SLD330-V and SLD390-V combine magazine and vacuum loading, with boards 0.6 to 4.5 mm. Magazine loading runs about 7 s per board and vacuum loading about 15 s.

Plan the side-two buffer

Size the number of magazines to the time between side one and side two. That buffer decides how often an operator changes magazines.

LED boards4 min read

Feeding 1.2 m LED Boards Without Bending Them

Long LED strips need long magazines, long pushers and support along their whole length.

Strips that bow in the middle

A 1.2 m LED board in a standard loader sags, catches on the conveyor gap and arrives at the printer out of position. Many LED makers end up feeding long boards by hand.

Why long boards need their own loader

Length multiplies every small problem: pusher alignment, rail parallelism, gaps between machines and the force needed to push a board out of a long magazine slot.

Support the board end to end

Use a magazine as long as the board, a pusher centred on it, and conveyors with close transfer gaps. For bare boards, use a long vacuum loader that lifts the board flat.

SLD350L and SUL350L

The SLD350L loader and SUL350L unloader take boards up to 1200 x 350 mm in 1200 x 460 x 563 mm stainless steel anti-static magazines, SMEMA compatible. They are 2800 and 3000 mm long and weigh 300 kg each. For bare long boards, the long SVL vacuum loader takes up to 1300 x 330 mm.

Check the whole line, not only the loader

A long-board loader helps only if the printer, mounter and oven also take the board. Check each machine's maximum length before you order.

Magazines4 min read

Magazines Matter as Much as the Loader

A loader is only as reliable as the magazine it pushes from. Bent racks and wrong pitch cause most loader jams.

The jam that was not the machine

An engineer spends an hour on a loader that pushes boards crooked, then finds one side plate of the magazine is bent. The loader was fine.

Why magazines wear out

Magazines are dropped, stacked, heated near ovens and adjusted hundreds of times. Slots wear, plates twist and adjustment screws loosen.

Standardise and inspect

Use one magazine standard per line, match the loader pitch to the slot pitch, and check width parallelism regularly. Use heat-resistant magazines where boards come straight from reflow or wave.

Our magazine racks

The S6601 to S6604 racks hold 50 boards at 10 mm pitch, from 355 x 320 x 563 mm up to 535 x 530 x 570 mm, with surface resistance 10e4 to 10e6 ohm. Standard types are rated 85 or 125 °C and heat-resistant types 120 °C and 160 to 200 °C. Screw and gear adjustment keeps the plates parallel.

Audit your magazines

Measure the width at the front and back of every magazine on one line. Replace any that differ by more than a millimetre.

Ready for AGVs and MES: The Connected Loader

The loader is where material meets the line. Make it the place where the line asks for material.

Operators running for magazines

In many factories an operator watches the loader, sees the last magazine and walks to the store. If they are late, the line stops.

Why the loader is the right signal

The loader knows exactly how many boards and magazines are left. That makes it the natural trigger for the next delivery, whether by person or by AGV.

Connect in steps

Start with sound and light alarms before starvation. Then send the signal to the shop-floor system. Finally let an AGV bring full magazines and take the empty ones away.

FAQ

Questions buyers ask before ordering

Which loader do I need?

It depends on how boards arrive. Boards in magazines need an SLD, SLF or S-MLD magazine loader. Bare boards in a stack need an SBU destacker or SVL vacuum loader. If you run both sides of a board, a combo loader takes both. Use the loader finder on this page with your board size.

What board sizes can you load?

Standard models run from 50 x 50 mm up to 530 x 460 mm. The SLD350L takes LED boards up to 1200 x 350 mm and the long vacuum loader up to 1300 x 330 mm. Other sizes are built to order.

Will it work with my printer or mounter?

Yes. Every loader uses the SMEMA interface at 900 ± 20 mm transfer height, left to right or right to left, so it connects to any brand of printer, mounter, insertion machine or conveyor.

How fast does a loader feed boards?

Magazine loaders feed about one board every 6 seconds; the combo loader is about 7 s from magazines and 15 s by vacuum. The downstream machine sets the real pace through SMEMA.

Which magazines can I use?

Standard ESD magazines from 355 x 320 x 563 mm to 535 x 530 x 570 mm, 50 slots at 10 mm pitch. We supply S6601 to S6604 racks, heat-resistant types up to 200 °C, and fit loaders to customer magazines.

Can you build a loader for flexible boards or lead frames?

Yes. The SFBL250 places flexible boards into pallets with a robot, and the SL600A feeds narrow semiconductor magazines. Custom designs take 5 days to 1 month.

How much space does a loader need?

The S-MLD250 mini loader is about 0.6 x 0.65 m. L-type loaders put the magazine beside the line in a 1.15 to 1.55 m square. Standard SLD loaders add 1.3 to 1.8 m of line length.

What support do you give?

Free installation and training, spare parts shipped within 1 day, 7 x 24 remote support and customised design from 5 days to 1 month.
