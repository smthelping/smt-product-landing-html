Vibratory bowl feeding - 3 x 3 mm to 50 x 50 mm - for SMT mounters, odd-form inserters and robotic cells

Bulk parts arrive in a bin.
They should reach the nozzle sorted, oriented and on time.

The SBF1001 bowl feeder is a bulk component supply platform. It takes loose electronic components, hardware, plastic parts and connectors from 3 x 3 mm up to 50 x 50 mm, weighing 10 g to 300 g, lifts them onto a vibrating track, screens them for direction and polarity where the material allows it, conveys them by direct vibration and air blowing, and presents them at a pick point with +/-0.5 mm precision. One feed takes 0.8 s. One bowl serves one material; two bowls in one frame serve two. It runs on 220 V, needs 0.4-0.6 MPa of plant air, and talks to the host machine through an 8-core aviation plug - or runs entirely on its own buttons.

01 - Why it matters

Bulk parts are where an automated line stops being automated

Every EMS plant we walk through has the same shape of problem: the pick-and-place side runs at machine speed, and the parts that arrive in a bag, a bin or a tray run at the speed of a wrist. The gap is almost never the placement head. It is the material presentation in front of it.

01

Bulk is the packaging nobody planned for

Reels and tubes have a standard interface. A bag of connectors, a bin of LED lenses, a tray of terminals and a box of hardware do not. So they get a bench, a pair of hands and a plastic scoop. That bench does not scale with your mounter - it scales with your headcount, and it is the first place orientation errors appear when a shift gets tired.

02

Orientation is the whole problem, not the transport

Moving a part is easy. Presenting it the same way every cycle is the hard part. A bowl feeder works because the bowl track is a mechanical filter: only parts travelling in the right attitude stay on the track. Everything else falls back into the bowl and tries again. That single idea is what turns an unordered bin into a stream a nozzle can pick.

03

One feeder per material is a feature, not a limit

A bowl track is machined for one component. That is why a bowl feeder is so repeatable - and why it will not accept an arbitrary part. Specify it honestly and it runs for years. Treat it as a universal machine and it will fail on the second material. The SBF1001 therefore comes as a single-bowl or a front-and-rear two-bowl frame: two materials, one footprint, two independent tracks.

02 - How it works

A bowl, a track and a stream of air

The SBF1001 is deliberately simple on the outside and precisely arranged on the inside. Loose material is loaded into the bowl. Coil-driven vibration lifts it up a helical track, where the track geometry sorts it into one attitude and rejects everything that does not fit. A separate vibrating rail carries the aligned parts out of the bowl, air blowing speeds them along and clears the track, an optical fibre confirms a part has arrived, and the component presents at the pick point. The host machine takes it from there.

  1. 01

    Load the bulk material

    Pour the loose components, hardware, plastic parts or connectors into the bowl. There is no tape, no reel and no tube to thread - that is the point of a bowl feeder. On a JUKI-style bowl feeder the bowl can be replenished while the mounter is still running.

  2. 02

    Vibrate, lift and sort

    Coil driving lifts the material up the helical track. The track is machined so that only correctly oriented parts survive the climb; anything upside down, on its side or doubled up falls back into the bowl and recirculates. Speed is set by voltage and frequency adjustment, not by guesswork.

  3. 03

    Screen for direction and polarity

    Where the material has a definable direction - a connector keyway, an LED lens with an axis, a polarised capacitor - sorting and polarity screening can be included as an option. It is selected per material, because it is a track feature, not a software setting.

  4. 04

    Trim the lead if the part needs it

    Lead cutting is a second documented option, also chosen according to the material. Bulk parts with leads often need the lead trimmed to a fixed length before the nozzle can take them cleanly. Where the part has no leads, this option is simply not specified.

  5. 05

    Convey on the rail by vibration and air

    Aligned parts leave the bowl onto a vibrating rail. Direct vibration carries them forward and air blowing accelerates them and keeps the track clear of debris and dust. The rail is the interface to the machine - on the four-rail LED version the whole rail set extending into the mounter is only 231 mm wide.

  6. 06

    Sense, position, present

    Optical fibre sensing confirms a part is at the pick point and holds the feed precision at +/-0.5 mm. The part is then presented for the nozzle, gripper or robot arm. If the part is not there, the feeder knows - it does not feed air and let the head pick nothing.

Two bowls, one frame

The front-and-rear frame carries two independent bowls and two rails in one 1120 mm footprint. Two materials run at the same time from one station, and each bowl keeps its own track geometry and its own optional polarity screening.

1120 x 460 x 910 mm - two bowls

A track that sorts by geometry

Sorting is not a sensor trick, it is machined into the track. Parts that present the wrong face fall back into the bowl. That is why the same feeder keeps presenting the part the same way on the first cycle and on the ten-thousandth.

Direction screening - option

Vibration and air, together

Direct vibration carries the part; air blowing accelerates it and keeps the rail clear. The combination is what allows a long rail run into the machine without the parts stalling or the track silting up with dust.

Direct vibration + air blow

Optical fibre, not a timer

Material positioning is optical fibre sensing rather than a count or a timer. The feeder knows whether a part is actually at the pick point, which is what makes +/-0.5 mm feed precision a real number instead of a best case.

+/-0.5 mm at the pick point

Coil drive, voltage and frequency control

The bowl is driven by coils and the speed gears are set by voltage and frequency adjustment. That is a continuous control, not a fixed stroke - which is how one platform covers a 10 g LED lens and a 300 g connector without changing hardware.

0.8 s per feed

It can run alone

Communication is IO over an 8-core, 16 mm aviation plug - or none at all. The feeder can be operated entirely from its own panel, which makes it usable on a non-standard machine, a robotic cell or a manual assembly station as easily as on a mounter.

IO or standalone

What the panel gives you

The feeder runs from a button panel with a PLC underneath. The same panel sets the vibration speed, checks the status and clears a fault - so a line operator can change a setting without opening a laptop.

  • Speed gears - set by voltage and frequency adjustment, giving stepless control of feed rate
  • Optical fibre sensing - confirms a part is present at the pick point before the head is told to pick
  • IO communication - the host machine can call for a feed, or the feeder can run entirely on its own buttons
  • Air regulation - working air at 0.4-0.6 MPa drives the blow-off that accelerates the part along the rail
  • Positioning key and clamping block - the mount is a mechanical datum, so a feeder can be swapped and re-seated square
  • 220 V supply at 3 A average and 5 A peak - no separate controller cabinet and no software licence

05 - Options

How the base platform is configured to your line

The SBF1001 is a platform, not a catalogue number. The list below separates what is genuinely configurable from what is fixed, so you can specify it once and correctly.

01

Single bowl or two bowls

One bowl for one material, or a front-and-rear frame carrying two independent bowls and two rails. The two-bowl version doubles the materials from one station without doubling the floor space.

02

Direction and polarity screening

Track features that reject incorrectly oriented parts and check polarity where the material has one. Selected per material at order, because it is machined into the track.

03

Lead cutting on the feeder

Trims the leads to length before the part reaches the pick point, so the nozzle takes a prepared component instead of a raw one. Also selected per material.

04

Multi-track and multi-channel bowls

For high-mix programmes, a documented multi-channel bowl is available. The BF200L vibratory bowl feeder is a compact double-layer plate with four tracks that supplies four kinds of material at the same time, at 220 V with a programmable control circuit. The BF220L is the companion model.

05

Standalone or host-communicated

Run it on its own buttons, or let the host machine drive it over IO through the 8-core aviation plug. Both modes ship in the same unit; the mode is a setting, not a different feeder.

06

Customised geometry and track

For parts outside the published window, or for a connector geometry that needs a dedicated rail, setter and separator, the bowl platform is customised. Southern Machinery documents a 1-month customised solution commitment for these builds.

06 - Set-up

Commissioning is a same-shift job

There is no software licence, no external compressor beyond the plant air line, and no calibration rig. Six steps take the feeder from the crate to producing parts, and the setting list is small enough to write on the inside of a cabinet door.

  1. 01

    Unbox and inspect

    Check the bowl, the rail, the power lead, the air line and the mounting hardware against the packing list. Confirm the bowl has not been distorted in transit - a bowl is a tuned mechanism, and a dent changes how it sorts.

  2. 02

    Mount on the positioning key

    Seat the feeder on its positioning key and clamp it with the clamping block. Other fixing structures are supported, but the key-and-block mount is a mechanical datum: it lets a feeder be removed, swapped and re-seated square without re-datuming the rail height.

  3. 03

    Power and air

    Connect the 220 V supply and bring the air line in at 0.4-0.6 MPa. Average current is 3 A with 5 A peak. If the host communicates with the feeder, connect the 8-core, 16 mm aviation plug. Never short the supply - that damages the control board.

  4. 04

    Load the material and set the rate

    Pour the bulk material into the bowl and set the speed gear by voltage and frequency adjustment. Start slow. A bowl that is over-driven throws parts off the track instead of sorting them, which looks like a tooling fault and is really a rate setting.

  5. 05

    Confirm the track and the pick point

    Run the bowl empty of everything except the target part and watch the track: only correctly oriented parts should reach the rail. Then confirm the optical fibre triggers at the pick point and that the part presents square to the nozzle before the head is allowed to pick.

  6. 06

    Validate before you run production

    Run the documented validation: a continuous feed test and a powered ageing run. Then clean the track and the fibre head, lock the settings, and log them per part number so the next changeover is a recall rather than a re-tune.

13 - FAQ

The questions we are actually asked

Straight answers, including the ones that end with us telling you not to buy.

What components can the SBF1001 bowl feeder feed?
Electronic components, hardware parts, plastic parts and connectors, from 3 x 3 mm up to 50 x 50 mm and 10 g to 300 g per piece. If your part is a loose, rigid part inside that window, it is very likely feedable - send a sample and we will confirm the track.
Can one bowl feeder run two different components?
Not in one bowl. The sorting track is machined for one component geometry, and that is precisely why the feeder is repeatable. What you can do is order the front-and-rear frame: two independent bowls, two rails, two materials, one 1120 x 460 x 910 mm station. Multi-channel bowls are also documented for four-material programmes such as LED lenses.
How fast is it, and what takt should I plan?
A feed takes 0.8 s, and feeding precision is +/-0.5 mm. In practice the placement head sets the takt - the feeder's job is to never starve it. Plan the takt from your head and your board, and treat the 0.8 s feed as headroom.
Will it handle polarity and orientation?
Orientation sorting is intrinsic - it is the track geometry, and any part presenting the wrong attitude falls back into the bowl. Polarity screening is a documented option selected according to the material, because it is a track feature and not a software setting. Send us the part and we will tell you which one you need.
What does it need from the plant?
220 V at 3 A average and 5 A peak, working air at 0.4-0.6 MPa, and a connection to the host machine through an 8-core, 16 mm aviation plug if you want IO communication. There is no external compressor beyond the plant air line, no software licence and no separate controller cabinet.
What happens when the bowl runs empty or a part jams?
Material positioning is optical fibre sensing, so the feeder knows whether a part is actually at the pick point rather than counting cycles. Most sorting problems are rate problems: a bowl that is over-driven throws parts off the track instead of sorting them. Reduce the speed gear first, then check the track for debris and the fibre head for dust.
What should I keep on the shelf?
Keep the wear items your own log tells you about, and rely on the documented 1-day spare-parts lead time rather than building a warehouse. The parts that are specific to you are the track parts - rail, setter and separator - so keep a record of the part numbers we made for your connector. The cheapest spare part is a setting you can find again.
