Automatic tray feeding - 40 trays behind one pick point - for SMT mounters, odd-form inserters and robotic cells

Tray parts arrive stacked in blister trays.
They should reach the head picked, oriented and presented.

The STP1001 is a servo-driven tray feeder that takes transformers, relays, hardware parts, memory module sockets and large capacitors from 3 x 3 x 3 mm up to 50 x 50 x 50 mm, lifts the magazine on a motor and screw rod, carries each tray to the pick position on a reciprocating belt, returns the empty tray to the lifting platform and presents the next one. It supplies one material every 3 seconds at +/-1 mm, holds 40 trays when the tray height is under 10 mm and 20 when it is over, and runs from a PLC with a touch screen - or from the host machine over IO.

01 - Why it matters

Tray parts are where a mixed-technology line quietly loses its afternoon

Ask any EMS plant what limits odd-form automation and the answer is usually the insertion machine. Ask what actually stops the cell and the answer is much more specific: the tray ran out, the part came up at the wrong height, the gripper could not reach it, somebody had to stop and reload. That is a material presentation problem wearing a machine problem's clothes.

01

The tray bench is the real bottleneck

Transformers, relays, DIMM sockets and large capacitors do not arrive on reels or in tubes. They arrive in blister trays, which means somebody has to open a tray, orient a part, hand it to the head and put the empty tray somewhere. That bench does not scale with your machine - it scales with your headcount, and it is the first place quality drifts when a shift gets tired.

02

Tray height is a capacity decision, not a detail

A tray feeder's real capacity is not how many trays it holds, it is how many it holds at your tray height. The STP1001 loads 40 trays when the tray is under 10 mm and 20 when it is over - the same magazine, half the autonomy. Measure the loaded tray height before you size the machine, because that one number decides how often somebody walks over to it.

03

Orientation is a process, not a step

A tray part is only insertable when it arrives at the pick position in the right attitude, at the right height, with the pins in a known state. Hand operators compensate for all three every cycle. A feeder that lifts, conveys, positions and shapes the pins turns those three into a setup - and a setup is the only version of that which survives the third shift.

02 - How it works

One magazine, one belt, and a pick point that never runs out of trays

The STP1001 is deliberately plain on the outside: a lifting platform on a motor and screw rod, a reciprocating conveyor belt, a loading magazine and an unloading path for empty trays. The sequence is what matters - the tray changes itself, the empty tray leaves, and the pick point stays fed without an operator standing next to it.

  1. 01

    Load the magazine by hand

    Open the rear safety door, pull out the tray carrier and place loaded trays into the magazine levels. Confirm each occupied level on the touch screen, then close the page and the door. Loading is a manual, once-per-shift job - everything after it is automatic.

  2. 02

    The tray changes itself

    Once running, the feeder indexes the magazine, moves the next tray to the pick position and continues. Tray change is part of the cycle rather than an operator task, which is the whole point: the cell does not stop because a tray emptied.

  3. 03

    The empty tray goes back

    After the last component is taken, the empty tray is conveyed by belt back to the lifting platform and out of the working path. Empty trays accumulate in one place instead of on the bench, and the pick position is cleared for the next tray automatically.

  4. 04

    The component is picked, not pushed

    A gripper or a nozzle takes the component from the tray. Which one is a parameter, not a different machine - Z1 and Z2 can each be set to nozzle or gripper on the parameter screen, so one feeder covers parts that need vacuum and parts that need clamping.

  5. 05

    The pins are shaped before they travel

    Tray parts usually need their pins straightened, corrected or formed before they can be inserted. The feeder does that as part of the transfer, so the part that arrives at the pick point is already in an insertable state rather than being fixed downstream by hand.

  6. 06

    The pick point stays fed

    The component is carried to the front-end reclaiming position, where the host machine takes it. Because the magazine, the belt and the empty-tray path all run from the same PLC, the pick point is refilled as part of the normal cycle. Feeding speed is 3 seconds per material at +/-1 mm.

05 - Family and options

Six tray platforms, and the six things that are genuinely configurable

The tray feeder is not one machine. It is a platform family that covers everything from a narrow 136 mm tray to a 2200 mm DIMM connector line, and the base machine is configured to your line rather than sold as a fixed unit. The table below is the documented family with the published envelope of each platform.

01

Tray height, measured first

The first configuration decision is the loaded tray height, because it sets capacity: 40 trays under 10 mm, 20 over. Measure it before you choose the magazine, not after.

02

Gripper or nozzle per station

Z1 and Z2 are individually set to nozzle or gripper. One machine can therefore handle a part that must be vacuum-picked and a part that must be clamped, without a second feeder.

03

Pin shaping on or off

Pin shaping and correcting run as part of the transfer and can be enabled or disabled per application, so a part that arrives already formed does not have to go through it.

04

Tray fixing structure

The standard fixing is a positioning key plus a clamping block. Other fixing structures are supported for trays that need a different location method.

05

Magazine count and rail delivery

The pick-up variant goes further: 9 pallets at 350 x 300 mm with a 4 s tray change and a 1.5 s pick-up cycle, and the rail variant delivers to the front-end reclaiming position instead of stopping at the pick point.

06 - Set-up

Commissioning is a same-shift job

There is no software licence and no external compressor beyond the plant air line. The feeder is a floor-standing unit, so the work is power, air, tray loading, parameters and a validation run. The parameters below are the ones the tray feeder manuals publish.

  1. 01

    Unpack and inspect

    Check the machine, the power lead, the air line and the tray carrier against the packing list. Confirm the tray carrier moves freely before power is applied, and that the safety doors close and latch.

  2. 02

    Position and level

    Set the machine at the working height your line needs and confirm it is level. The feeder is fixed with a positioning key plus a clamping block, and other fixing structures are supported for non-standard mounts.

  3. 03

    Power and air

    Connect 220 V single phase - average current 5 A, peak 8 A - and bring the air line in at 0.4-0.6 MPa. On power-up the motor may alarm out of origin; press reset to home the machine before running.

  4. 04

    Load the magazine

    Open the rear safety door, pull out the tray carrier and place loaded trays into the magazine levels. Confirm each occupied level on the touch screen, then close the page and the door. Remember the capacity rule: 40 trays under 10 mm, 20 over.

  5. 05

    Set the parameters

    Set the axis speeds, the acceleration and deceleration time, the cylinder and gripper delays, and the Z1 and Z2 tool selection. Then load the parameters and write them to the PLC - a parameter that is edited but not written is not in effect.

  6. 06

    Validate before you run production

    Run the documented validation: a continuous feeding test and a powered ageing run, as recorded in the inspection records. Then log the parameter set per part number, so the next changeover is a lookup rather than an experiment.

10 - Insights

Eight notes on tray feeding, written for the person who has to make the number

Each note follows the same path: the moment the problem becomes visible, why the obvious explanation is usually wrong, a method you can run this week, the machine that carries the method, the habit that keeps it working, and the one action that starts it. Open a note to read it.

NOTE 01The tray bench is not a headcount problem

Every mixed-technology line has a bench where somebody opens a blister tray, orients a transformer and hands it to the head. It is usually treated as a staffing issue. It is a material presentation issue wearing a staffing costume.

NOTE 02Your odd-form cell is not slow. Its tray change is.

When an odd-form cell misses its output target the insertion head gets blamed. Most of the time the head is fine and somebody is walking trays in and out of it.

NOTE 03The six-number tray audit that decides the purchase

Before you price a tray feeder, price the problem. Six measurements taken at the bench will tell you whether a tray feeder pays for itself - and whether you should buy one at all.

NOTE 04Tray height, component envelope, gripper gap: three numbers that decide your tray yield

Tray feeding problems are usually described as quality problems. They are almost always three mechanical numbers that were never specified together.

NOTE 05Stop buying placement machines. Start buying material presentation.

Plants that struggle with odd-form automation usually already own the machine. What they do not own is a reliable way to present the part to it.

NOTE 06From two shifts at the tray bench to one: the arithmetic

Labour reduction is the easiest claim in this industry to make and the easiest to disprove. So here is the arithmetic, laid out so you can attack it with your own numbers.

NOTE 07Transformers, relays and DIMM sockets on one feeder: planning the mix

The parts on a tray bench are rarely one family. They are transformers, relays, hardware, memory module sockets and large capacitors - and they do not all want the same handling.

13 - FAQ

The questions we are actually asked

Straight answers, including the ones that end with us telling you not to buy.

What components can the STP1001 feed?
Transformers, relays, hardware parts, memory module sockets and large capacitors, with component outlines from 3 x 3 x 3 mm up to 50 x 50 x 50 mm, presented on blister trays from 150 x 150 mm up to 200 x 300 mm in PET plastic film, loaded to 3 kg or less.
How many trays does it hold?
40 trays when the loaded tray height is under 10 mm, and 20 trays when it is over 10 mm. This is the most common specification mistake we see: a customer quotes the 40-tray figure and then loads a 15 mm tray. Measure the loaded tray height - base to the highest point of the component - before you size the magazine.
How fast is it, and what takt should I plan?
Feeding speed is 3 seconds to supply one material, and feeding precision is +/-1 mm. In practice the insertion head or the robot sets the takt; the feeder's job is to never starve it. Plan the feeder for the worst-case tray change, not the average pick.
Does it pick with vacuum or with a gripper?
Both. Z1 and Z2 are each set to nozzle or gripper on the parameter screen, so a part that must be vacuum-picked and a part that must be clamped can run on the same machine without a second feeder.
What does it need from the plant?
220 V single phase at 5 A average and 8 A peak, working air at 0.4-0.6 MPa, and a connection to the host machine through an 8-core, 16 mm diameter aviation plug if you want IO communication. It also runs standalone with no communication at all.
What happens when a tray empties or a component is missing?
The feeder counts picks. The no-material count parameter sets how many gripper picks are used to judge whether material is present before the feeder reports no material, and the delay parameters govern the gripper, vacuum and tray sensors. Most tray feeding problems are set-up problems: a tray that is not seated on the positioning key, or a delay that has not been tuned to the tray weight.
What should I keep on the shelf?
The wear items your own log tells you about - grippers, nozzles and the belts and sensors the machine uses - plus the documented 1-day spare-parts lead time as your safety net. A tray feeder is a material handling machine, so the consumables are the parts that touch the tray and the component, not the servos.
How do I get a quotation?
Send one loaded tray of each component, the component outline in millimetres, the loaded tray height, the tray drawing and material, your machine model, and your monthly board volume. We will confirm the tray format, the pick method for Z1 and Z2, the shaping requirement and the magazine count before anyone quotes a price.
