How it works

Six steps from reel to board

What the feeder does, and where the host mounter takes over.

1

Load the tape

Radial parts on a single carrier tape, from reel or ammo box, are threaded through the guide.

Tape thickness 2 mm max.
2

Index one pitch

A pneumatic pawl moves the tape by one transport hole each cycle.

P0 = 12.7 or 15 mm, hole Ø 4 mm.
3

Cut the leads

The cutter trims both leads at the set distance above the transport holes.

A = 11-17 mm, ±0.2 mm.
4

Form if needed

The bend version folds the leads 90° for tall parts that must lie down.

S with 90° bend version.
5

Signal part ready

The feeder presents the part at a fixed pickup point and exchanges a DC 24 V signal with the machine.

Air-driven, DC 24 V input.
6

Pick and insert

The mounter's nozzle or gripper takes the part and inserts the leads into the PCB.

Host program, nozzle and vision set per part.

Insights

Eight short reads on feeding radial parts

Practical articles for SMT and THT process engineers.

Process5 min read

Why radial parts are still inserted by hand next to a fast SMT line

Many lines place every SMD part by machine, then send the board to a row of people who push in capacitors and LEDs. A radial feeder moves that work onto the mounter.

The bottleneck after the oven

Walk along a typical mixed-technology line. The mounters place thousands of chip parts an hour, the oven runs at full speed, and then the boards queue at a manual insertion table where four or five people push electrolytic capacitors, LEDs and varistors into holes by hand. That table sets the real output of the line.

Why it stays manual

Radial parts arrive on carrier tape, not on embossed SMD tape. A standard mounter feeder cannot index that tape, cannot cut the leads and cannot hold a tall body still for the nozzle. So the parts stay with people, and so do the mistakes: reversed polarity, missing parts and bent leads.

What has to change

To let the mounter handle a radial part, something must step the tape one transport hole at a time, trim both leads to the length the PCB hole needs and present the body at a fixed point the nozzle can find every cycle. That is the job of a radial taped feeder.

How the S-RF1001 does it

The S-RF1001 sits in the mounter's feeder bank. It is 550 x 110 x 130 mm, air-driven and talks to the machine through a DC 24 V input signal. It accepts tape with 12.7 or 15 mm pitch and lead spacing up to 25 mm, and the brochure shows it working on YAMAHA, JUKI and UNIVERSAL machines.

A first step for this week

Count the radial part numbers on your three highest-volume boards and note how many placements each board needs. Parts with the highest count and the simplest leads are the first candidates to move onto the mounter.

Talk to us

Send the part list and two or three metres of taped samples to info@smthelp.com. We will tell you which parts the feeder can take and what the mounter needs.

Engineering6 min read

Reading a radial tape: eleven dimensions that decide feeding

Before any feeder is chosen, the tape has to be measured. The S-RF1001 brochure lists eleven dimensions, from transport hole to total thickness.

A feeder that jammed on day two

A plant bought feeders on the strength of a part number. On the second day of production a new reel arrived from another supplier, the body sat 3 mm higher on the tape, and the nozzle started missing parts. Nothing was wrong with the feeder. The tape had changed.

Why tape matters more than the part

The feeder never sees the data sheet of the capacitor. It sees the carrier tape: where the transport holes are, how far the body sits from them and how thick the whole package is. Two suppliers of the same capacitor can tape it differently.

The eleven numbers

Measure A, the transport hole to cutting edge; B and C, the clearances to the crimp and carrier tape; D0, the hole diameter; d, the lead wire; F, the lead spacing; P0, the hole pitch; H, H0 and H1, the heights to the body bottom, crimp and body top; and t, the total thickness.

The S-RF1001 limits

The brochure gives A as 11 to 17 mm with ±0.2 mm, D0 as 4 mm, lead wire up to 0.8 mm, lead spacing up to 25 mm, P0 of 12.7 or 15 mm, H at least 18 mm, H0 at least 15 mm, H1 up to 40 mm and total thickness up to 2 mm. The checker on this page compares your figures with these limits.

What to do today

Take one reel from each supplier you use for a radial part and measure these eleven values with calipers. Write them on one sheet. If they differ, that is the variation the feeder must handle.

Send us the sheet

Email the measurements and a short length of each tape to info@smthelp.com and we will confirm the feeder setting for every supplier.

Engineering5 min read

Cut length: getting the leads right for the PCB hole

A radial part that is cut too long fouls the clinch area; cut too short and it falls out before soldering. The cutting distance is set on the feeder.

Parts lifting out in the wave

An engineer saw radial capacitors rising out of their holes as boards entered the wave solder machine. The parts were inserted correctly, but the leads were so short that nothing held them while the board moved.

Why cut length drives quality

On a hand line people cut and bend leads by feel. On a machine, every part must have the same lead length, measured from a fixed reference on the tape, or the insertion depth and the solder joint vary from board to board.

How to set it

Start from the PCB: board thickness plus the protrusion you want under the board gives the lead length below the body. Then convert that into the cutting distance from the transport holes, using the H and A values of the tape.

What the feeder offers

The S-RF1001 tape table gives the transport hole to cutting edge distance A as 11 to 17 mm with a tolerance of ±0.2 mm, adjustable. It also keeps at least 2 mm between the cutting edge and the crimp and at least 4 mm to the carrier tape, so the cut stays clear of the tape.

A quick check

Pull ten parts from today's hand line and measure the lead length below the body. If the spread is more than 1 mm, a fixed machine cut will already improve your solder joints.

Ask for a cut test

Send your PCB thickness and hole drawing with the samples. We cut a set of parts on the feeder and return them with measurements.

Application5 min read

When a radial part needs a 90° bend

Tall capacitors and some LEDs must lie flat on the board or point sideways. A bend version of the feeder forms both leads after cutting.

The capacitor that did not fit the housing

A power supply design had an electrolytic capacitor standing 30 mm tall, but the plastic cover left only 15 mm. The answer was to lay the capacitor on its side, which meant every lead had to be bent by hand before insertion.

Why bending is hard by hand

A hand bend changes with the operator. The body ends up at different heights, the leads get scratched, and the bend point moves, so the part no longer lines up with the holes. Rework follows.

Doing it on the feeder

The cleanest place to bend is right after the cut, while the part is still held in a known position. A forming block folds both leads at the same point every cycle, and the nozzle picks a part that is already in its final shape.

The S version with 90° bend

Our Radial Taped Feeder S with 90° bend, shown in the 3D drawing on this page, adds that forming step to the radial feeder. Bend position and body size are set for each part, so we confirm them with your samples.

Find the candidates

List the radial parts on your boards that are bent before insertion today, and note the bend direction against the polarity mark. Those are the parts for the bend version.

Send the drawing

Email the part drawing with the bend you need to info@smthelp.com. We return a 3D check of the formed part against your PCB.

Application5 min read

Large capacitors: body size, grip and the L-S feeder

Big electrolytic capacitors are heavy and wide. They need a feeder that holds the body steady and leaves room for a gripper nozzle.

A nozzle that dropped every tenth part

A line tried to pick 16 mm capacitors with a vacuum nozzle. The round top and the weight of the part meant the nozzle lost about one part in ten, usually just above the board.

Why size changes everything

A large body swings on its leads when the tape moves. If it is not held at the pickup point, the nozzle meets it at a slightly different place each time. Vacuum also struggles on round, heavy tops, so a gripper is often the better tool.

What the feeder must provide

Guide rails that support the body, a pickup point with space for gripper fingers around the part, and a cut that does not twist the part as the leads are separated.

The L-S capacitor version

Our Radial Taped Feeder L-S, shown in the second 3D drawing, has a taller housing and a wider guide for large capacitors. Within the S-RF1001 tape table, the body top can sit up to 40 mm above the transport holes; wider bodies are reviewed per part.

Measure your largest part

Take the largest radial capacitor on your boards and note its diameter, height and weight. That single part usually decides which feeder version you need.

Get a gripper proposal

Send the part and your mounter model. We suggest the feeder version and a nozzle or gripper for the pick.

Integration6 min read

Mounting a radial feeder on YAMAHA, JUKI and UNIVERSAL machines

The feeder has to fit the machine's feeder bank, reach the pickup area and exchange a ready signal. The brochure shows three working solutions.

A feeder that arrived without a home

A buyer ordered radial feeders before checking the mounter. When they arrived, the bank had no free slots wide enough, the cables had no connector and the machine program had no way to wait for the part.

Why integration comes first

Every mounter brand has its own feeder bank, height, pickup window and signal scheme. A radial feeder is wider than a standard 8 mm feeder, so slot count and position matter as much as the feeder itself.

Three things to confirm

First the mechanical fit: slot, clamp and the height of the pickup point. Second the signal: how the machine knows a part is ready. Third the program: pickup coordinates, nozzle and vision settings for a part with leads.

What has been done

The S-RF1001 brochure shows solutions on YAMAHA, JUKI and UNIVERSAL machines and lists eleven brands in all. The feeder is air-driven and uses a DC 24 V input signal. Machines other than the three shown need a review for the exact model.

Prepare the machine data

Note your mounter model and year, the number of free feeder slots, the nozzle types you have and whether the machine can take a gripper. A photo of the feeder bank helps.

Integration4 min read

Air and signal: what a pneumatic radial feeder needs

The S-RF1001 runs on compressed air and a DC 24 V signal. Getting both right avoids most start-up problems.

A slow feeder that was not slow

During a trial, the feeder seemed to miss every few cycles. The cause was a long, thin air line shared with three other devices. When the pressure dipped, the indexing pawl did not finish its stroke.

Why utilities matter

A pneumatic feeder moves the tape, cuts and forms with air. Its timing depends on steady pressure and clean, dry air. Its handshake with the mounter depends on a clean DC 24 V signal.

What to plan

A dedicated air drop with a filter and regulator near the feeder bank, short tubing, and a signal cable routed away from motor cables. Agree with the machine programmer which signal starts a feed and how the machine waits.

What the S-RF1001 needs

According to the brochure the feeder is air-driven and takes a DC 24 V input signal. The pressure range and the exact signal wiring are set for each host machine during the mounting review.

Check your air today

Look at the air supply at the mounter where the feeder would sit. Note the pressure, the filter condition and how many other users share the line.

Include it in the review

Add a photo of the air drop to the machine details you send us, and we will include the utility list in the proposal.

Buying guide5 min read

A sample test plan before you order radial feeders

A good test uses your own tape, your own board and agreed pass criteria. Here is the plan we follow.

A demo that proved nothing

A supplier ran a beautiful demo with its own capacitors on its own tape. The buyer's parts, from a different source with a different tape, behaved quite differently, and the order was cancelled after delivery.

Why your samples matter

Feeding depends on the exact tape, lead stiffness and body shape. Only your parts on your tape can show how the feeder will run in your factory.

The plan

Choose three part numbers: the most common, the largest and the hardest. Send two or three metres of original tape for each, the tape and part drawings with polarity, the lead length you need, the PCB hole spacing and the mounter model.

What we test

We set the S-RF1001 or the bend or L-S version, cut and present the parts, measure the lead length and check that the pickup point repeats. We record a video and report any part that falls outside the tape table.

Agree the pass criteria

Before the test, agree what counts as a pass: lead length tolerance, number of parts fed without a stop and the condition of the leads. Write it down so both sides judge the same result.

FAQ

Questions buyers ask before ordering

What does the S-RF1001 do?

It feeds radial lead taped parts into an SMT mounter: it indexes the tape, cuts the leads to length and presents each part at a pickup point, so the mounter can insert them like any other part.

Which mounters does it work with?

The brochure shows working solutions on YAMAHA, JUKI and UNIVERSAL machines. It lists eleven brands in total; any machine outside the three shown needs a mounting and signal review for its exact model.

What tape does it accept?

Single carrier tape with Ø 4 mm transport holes at 12.7 or 15 mm pitch, lead wire up to 0.8 mm, lead spacing up to 25 mm, body top up to 40 mm above the holes and tape thickness up to 2 mm.

Can it bend the leads?

Yes, the S version with 90° bend folds both leads after cutting so a tall part can lie on the board. Bend size is set for each part.

What utilities does it need?

Compressed air for the drive and a DC 24 V input signal from the mounter. The feeder measures 550 x 110 x 130 mm.

How fast does it feed?

No feeding speed is published. We measure it on your parts and your machine during the sample test, because the mounter's pick and insert cycle usually sets the pace.

Is it an insertion machine?

No. It is a feeder. Insertion is done by the host mounter with a suitable nozzle or gripper, and clinching, if needed, is handled separately.
