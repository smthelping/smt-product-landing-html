How SRT7001 Works in Your THT Flow

1. Reel Loading & Tape Guidance

The operator mounts the terminal reel on the shaft defined in the 3D model, routes the tape through the guide track, and locks it into the clamping mechanism at the pick zone.[web:15][web:93]

  • Reel‑based terminal carrier maintains consistent pitch and orientation.[web:3][web:93]
  • Guides and bushings minimize tape skew and vertical play at the pick mouth.[web:64][web:75]

2. Indexing to a Fixed Pick Point

An internal drive indexes the tape one pitch at a time, always bringing the next terminal to the same pick reference point according to the SRT7001 CAD geometry.[web:4][web:86]

  • Pitch accuracy supports ±0.5mm pick position at the terminal center.[web:64][web:75]
  • Presence and empty sensors protect against mis‑picks and empty feed conditions.[web:64][web:69]

3. Pick, Insert, and Wave Solder

With custom nozzles or grippers, your odd‑form head or robot picks from the SRT7001 pick point, inserts terminals into THT holes, then sends the PCB to wave solder as part of a standard THT line.[web:49][web:54][file:47]

  • Brackets and reference points derived from the 3D model help align X/Y/Z for JUKI, Panasonic, Yamaha or dedicated odd‑form platforms.[web:3][web:87]
  • Simple I/O logic (start/ready/error) integrates with existing line controls.[file:47][web:54]
SRT7001 converts terminal reels into a stable, machine‑friendly supply, allowing your insertion head to run at its designed performance instead of waiting for operators.
