Why EMS Lines Need a Reel Terminal Feeder

Typical Challenges in THT Terminal Insertion

  • Manual feeding of FASTON / tab terminals from loose bags or trays leads to unstable takt time and operator fatigue on high-volume lines.[web:3][file:47]
  • Irregular odd-form leads and terminal geometry are difficult to handle with standard SMT tape feeders.[web:54][web:55]
  • Changeover between terminal variants often requires full mechanical re-setup, creating non-productive time.[file:47]

SRT7001 is designed to convert terminal reels into a stable, machine-readable feeding process that can be synchronized with odd-form insertion heads or pick-and-place robots.

Typical Boards & Scenarios

  • Power supply PCB with multiple FASTON terminals and heavy connectors.
  • Automotive harness / relay boards requiring mixed THT relays, tabs, and terminals.[file:47]
  • Industrial control boards where THT connectors must be inserted reliably before wave soldering.[file:47]

SRT7001 can be installed on a dedicated odd-form inserter or integrated as an auxiliary feeder on compatible SMT platforms, depending on the line concept.[web:3][web:54][file:47]

How SRT7001 Works in THT Odd-Form Assembly

1. Reel Loading & Indexing

  • Operators mount terminal reels (e.g. FASTON on tape) on the feeder’s reel shaft and route the tape through the guiding and clamping mechanism.[web:3][web:54]
  • A stepper or servo-driven indexing mechanism advances the tape by one pitch per cycle, ensuring each terminal is presented at the exact pick-up point.[web:3][web:54]
  • Integrated sensors monitor terminal presence and pitch to avoid mis-picks and empty feed conditions.[web:3][web:54]

2. Pick-Up with Customized Nozzle / Gripper

  • Southern Machinery designs customized nozzles or grippers matching the geometry of each terminal type in the catalog, ensuring secure pick-up without deformation.[web:54][web:55][file:47]
  • The insertion head (odd-form machine, robot, or compatible SMT head) approaches the pick position, engages the terminal, and retracts with controlled force.[web:3][web:54]
  • Pick confirmation can be achieved via vacuum sensing or dedicated I/O signals, depending on the host platform.

3. Insertion & Wave Soldering Flow

  • The system inserts the terminal into the THT hole pattern on the PCB with controlled insertion speed and depth, coordinated by the host machine.[file:47]
  • After insertion, the board proceeds to wave soldering as part of a complete THT line including auto-insertion, conveyors, and wave soldering machines from Southern Machinery.[file:47]
  • This integrated flow reduces manual handling and ensures consistent terminal positioning before soldering.

4. Electrical & Mechanical Integration

  • SRT7001 exchanges basic I/O signals (start / ready / error / sensor state) with the host controller so that feeding is synchronized with the pick-and-insert sequence.[web:3][web:54]
  • Mechanical brackets are customized per machine brand (e.g. JUKI or dedicated odd-form platforms) so the feeder sits at the correct angle and Z-height for reliable pick-up.[web:3][web:54]
