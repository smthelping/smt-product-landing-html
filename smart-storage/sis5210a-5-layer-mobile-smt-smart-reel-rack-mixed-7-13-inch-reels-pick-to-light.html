How it works

Six steps from reel store to feeder

The SIS5210A uses the same software and slot lighting as our fixed racks. What changes is that the rack itself travels with the kit.

1

Set the layer mix

Decide how many half-layers carry 7-inch reels and how many carry 13-inch reels. Slot width is adjusted to the reels you actually run.

The product drawing shows four 265 mm layers and one 415 mm bottom layer for large reels.
2

Scan and put away

A PDA or fixed scanner reads the reel label. The rack lights a free slot of the right size and records the slot against the Reel ID.

No fixed home per part number, so the rack fills evenly.
3

Load the kit by light

The ERP or MES releases a work order. The software compares it with stock and lights every reel needed, each order in its own colour, oldest date code first.

Seven LED colours per slot; tower lights on both columns.
4

Roll it to the line

The rack runs on casters, with a battery inside and Wi-Fi on top. The kit travels as one unit instead of being unloaded onto a separate cart.

Function on the parameter sheet: transport.
5

Pick and verify at the feeder

At the line, the slots stay lit. Each reel is scanned out and checked against the feeder station before the line starts.

Station tables can be imported from Excel.
6

Return partial reels

Partial reels go back into any free lit slot with their new quantity, ready to be used first next time. The rack is then recharged or sent for the next kit.

Partial-reel management is part of the warehouse software.

Mixed reel sizes5 min read

Half Your Reel Store Is Empty, and the Other Half Is Full

Separate shelves for 7-inch and 13-inch reels look tidy on a drawing. On the floor, one size always runs out of room while the other sits half empty.

Two shelves, two different problems

Walk into most SMT reel stores and you will find the same picture: rows of narrow shelving for 7-inch reels, a separate area for 13-inch reels, and an overflow table where the reels that did not fit end up. The table is the most honest part of the store. It shows the exact moment the planned split stopped matching the real product mix.

Why a fixed split goes wrong

Your mix of reel sizes is set by the boards you build, and that changes every quarter. A new power board brings large reels of inductors and connectors; a new controller brings hundreds of small passives. Shelving bought for last year's mix cannot follow. Space is lost twice: empty slots for one size, and overflow, search time and mistakes for the other.

Let the slot follow the reel

The fix is to stop deciding the size split when you buy the shelving. Plan storage in small blocks that can carry either size, so the rack can be rebalanced when the mix shifts. Keep every reel addressable whatever its size, so a large reel is found as quickly as a small one.

How the SIS5210A handles both sizes

The SIS5210A holds 600 seven-inch reels or 300 thirteen-inch reels on a 1320 x 400 mm footprint, and the two sizes can be mixed freely across its five layers. Slot width is adjustable, every slot has its own LED, and the same software tracks both sizes by Reel ID, part number and date code. The standard drawing uses four 265 mm layers for 7-inch reels and a 415 mm bottom layer for 13-inch reels.

Count your mix before you buy

Pull a month of kit lists and count how many 7-inch and 13-inch reels each kit actually uses, at peak and on an average day. That ratio, not the current shelving, should set the first layer plan. Recount after six months and adjust the slot widths if the mix has moved.

Line-side kitting5 min read

The Kit Cart Is Where Good Kitting Goes to Die

You can light every reel in the store and still lose the gain the moment reels are unloaded onto an ordinary cart.

The weakest link sits on four wheels

Plants that invest in pick-to-light racks often keep the old kit cart. The reels are picked quickly and correctly, then dropped onto a plain trolley, pushed to the line and sorted again by hand at the feeders. Every reel is handled one more time, and the trolley has no idea what it is carrying.

What happens between store and line

Once a reel leaves a smart rack for a plain cart, it is invisible. Reels slide between orders, a partial reel is left on the cart for the next shift, and the operator at the line repeats the search the warehouse just finished. The data stops at the warehouse door, exactly where the line needs it.

Make the kit and the rack the same thing

Keep the reels in lit, addressed slots all the way to the feeder. If the rack that holds the kit can travel, there is no unload, no second sort and no blind spot. The operator at the line sees the same lights the picker saw, and every reel still has its address.

Rolling the SIS5210A to the line

The SIS5210A is built for this job. Its parameter table lists its function as transport. It runs on casters, carries a battery inside and has a Wi-Fi controller on top, so the slot lights keep working while it moves. Three work orders can be lit in three colours, picked into the rack and wheeled to the line as one unit.

Time one changeover end to end

Measure a normal changeover from work-order release to the first board, and split it into picking, loading the cart, moving, sorting at the line and verifying. The loading and sorting minutes are what a mobile rack removes. That is the number to put into the payback worksheet.

High-mix production5 min read

High-Mix Lines Need Storage That Changes as Fast as the Product

Ten changeovers a day, small batches, prototypes on Friday. Storage designed for long runs quietly becomes the bottleneck.

Changeovers are the new production time

Contract manufacturers with many customers now run more changeovers than ever. A line that once ran one product all week now runs five or ten jobs a day, each with its own kit of reels in different sizes. The placement machines cope. The material flow around them often does not.

Long-run storage in a short-run world

Big fixed racks are excellent for stocking thousands of reels. They are less good at serving many small kits fast, especially when each kit mixes small passives with large reels of connectors or inductors. Pickers walk between areas for each size, and new product introductions get squeezed in wherever space remains.

Small, flexible stock near the line

Keep the bulk stock in the warehouse and add small, flexible racks that carry the next few jobs. Each rack should take any reel size, light the reels for the next job and move to the line that needs it. That gives a buffer that follows the schedule rather than the floor plan.

The SIS5210A as a job buffer

With five layers, 600 seven-inch or 300 thirteen-inch reels and free mixing of both sizes, the SIS5210A can hold several upcoming kits at once. Each job lights in its own colour. Because it shares software and API with our SIS7200A warehouse racks, reels move between the main store and the buffer without losing their history.

Map tomorrow's jobs, not last year's

List tomorrow's jobs and count the reels each needs by size. If several jobs share many part numbers, one buffer rack can serve them all. Try it on one line for a month before deciding how many racks to add.

Battery and Wi-Fi4 min read

Why Your Smart Rack Should Not Need a Power Socket

Every cable on a moving rack is a reason not to move it. Wireless racks change where storage can live on the floor.

The cable decides where the rack stands

A smart rack that needs mains power and a network cable ends up wherever the sockets are. That is usually against a wall in the warehouse, far from where the reels are used. Moving it means an electrician, and so it never moves.

Fixed power creates fixed habits

Lines get rebalanced, new lines arrive and cells are rearranged. Storage tied to cables cannot follow, so the distance between reels and feeders grows over time. Operators fill the gap with plain carts, and the traceability the rack provides stops at the cable's length.

Power and network that travel

A rack that carries its own battery and talks over Wi-Fi can be parked wherever the work is. Charging becomes a routine, like charging handheld scanners, and the rack's position becomes a planning choice instead of a building constraint.

What the SIS5210A carries

The SIS5210A lists DC 5 V, 0.06 kW power with the battery inside, and wired and wireless networking. The drawing shows the battery in the base and the Wi-Fi controller on top, between the two tower lights. The software and HTTP + JSON API are the same as for our fixed racks, so your MES does not need to know whether a rack is moving.

Check coverage and charging first

Before rollout, walk the route from store to line with a phone and check Wi-Fi coverage. Ask us for the battery capacity and charging time, and decide where racks will charge between shifts. These two checks avoid almost every surprise on day one.

Capacity planning5 min read

How Many 7-Inch and 13-Inch Slots Do You Really Need?

Most reel stores are sized by guess and by the last purchase. A simple count of reels per kit gives a better answer in an afternoon.

The number everyone guesses

Ask a plant how many reel slots it needs and the answer is usually the number it has, plus some. Ask how many of those should be 13-inch and the answer is a shrug. Yet that split decides whether storage works or overflows.

Why totals mislead

Total reel counts hide the mix. A store can have plenty of slots overall and still overflow because every new job brings large reels. Sizing by totals also ignores where reels are used: the warehouse needs bulk capacity, the line needs a few kits close at hand.

Size by kit, then by store

Start from the kit. Count the reels in your largest common kits by size. Multiply by the number of kits you want staged near the lines at once. That gives line-side capacity. Then size the warehouse for the remaining stock, with headroom for growth.

Turning the count into SIS5210A racks

One SIS5210A holds 600 seven-inch or 300 thirteen-inch reels. In practice each 7-inch half-layer gives 60 slots and each 13-inch half-layer gives 30, so a rack with four 7-inch layers and a 13-inch bottom layer gives 480 small and 60 large slots. The 3D planner on this page lets you try your own split and read the capacity live.

Leave room for change

Plan line-side racks at about 80 to 85 percent occupancy so put-away always finds a free slot. Review the split after each major new product, and adjust slot widths rather than buying a new rack.

Error proofing4 min read

The Last Ten Metres Are Where Wrong Reels Get Loaded

Most error proofing stops at the warehouse. Most wrong reels are loaded at the line.

Right kit, wrong feeder

A kit can leave the warehouse perfectly checked and still produce a scrapped batch. At the line, reels are sorted by hand, two look-alike reels swap places, or a 13-inch reel of the right part goes to the wrong slot of a feeder bank. The error was made in the last ten metres.

Checks that end too early

Scanning out of the store proves the kit was right when it left. It does not prove which reel went to which feeder. Between the two scans the reels are just plastic on a cart, and every handover is a chance to swap them.

Keep the reel lit until it is loaded

Error proofing works best when the reel is guided until the moment it is mounted. Light the reel the operator should take next, scan it against the feeder station, and alarm on any reel taken or placed out of turn.

How the SIS5210A guards the line side

Because the SIS5210A travels with the kit, its slot LEDs stay active at the line. Reels light in order colours, each reel is scanned out and checked against the feeder station, and a reel placed where no slot was lit makes the slot flash and the tower light alarm. The 3D model on this page shows a wrong-size alarm in action.

Find your own near misses

Look at the last six months of material-related defects and line stops. Note where each was caught and where it started. If most began at the line, the next investment belongs there, not in the warehouse.

Store layout5 min read

One Big Store or Many Small Racks? Use Both

Fixed high-density racks and mobile mixed-size racks solve different problems. Plants that pick one often regret the other.

A debate that is usually settled too early

When a plant plans a smart reel store, the conversation quickly becomes a choice: one large central store, or racks spread along the lines. Each camp has good arguments, and the decision is often made before anyone counts the reels.

What each approach misses

A central store gives density and control but adds walking and a separate kitting step. Racks spread along the lines are close to the work but duplicate stock and are hard to keep accurate. Neither alone fits a plant with many lines and a changing mix.

Centre for stock, edge for kits

Use high-density fixed racks for bulk stock and mobile racks for the kits that are about to run. Reels flow from centre to edge as jobs are released, and partial reels flow back. One software system tracks both, so a reel never drops out of sight.

SIS7200A at the centre, SIS5210A at the edge

The SIS7200A holds 1,400 seven-inch reels on a 2130 x 400 mm footprint, ideal for the central store. The SIS5210A holds 600 seven-inch or 300 thirteen-inch reels in any mix and rolls to the line. Both use the same warehouse software and HTTP + JSON API, so the ERP or MES sees one store.

Start with the busiest line

Pilot the combination on the line with the most changeovers. Measure kitting time and line-side errors for a month, then decide how many fixed and mobile racks the rest of the plant needs.

Payback4 min read

How to Test a 6-Month Payback Claim Before You Believe It

Product sheets love round numbers. Here is how to turn a brochure claim into a figure your finance team will sign.

Round numbers invite questions

The SIS5210A product sheet states a 90 percent labour reduction and a six-month return. We print those claims on this page, and we also tell you we have no SIS5210A customer case to back them yet. That is exactly why you should test them with your own numbers.

Where brochure paybacks go wrong

Published paybacks are usually measured at the best site, on the best line, counting every saving at once. Your plant may run fewer kits, pay different wages or already have part of the benefit. Copying the figure into a business case invites a hard conversation a year later.

Build it from your own minutes

Count the people who handle reels between store and line today, and how much of their time goes to searching, loading carts, sorting at the line and rechecking. Decide honestly how much of that a mobile pick-to-light rack would remove. Multiply by wage and shifts, and compare with the quoted price.

Applying it to the SIS5210A

The worksheet on this page does exactly that: staff today, staff released, monthly cost, shifts and quoted price. It uses only labour, so savings from fewer wrong reels, fewer line stops and lower safety stock come on top. If your answer is close to six months, the claim holds for you; if not, you know why.

Agree the measure before you buy

Write down the measurements you will repeat after go-live: kitting time per order, staff per shift, wrong-reel incidents. Measure them for two weeks before and after. That turns the payback into a fact rather than a promise.

FAQ

Questions buyers ask before ordering

Can 7-inch and 13-inch reels really share one rack?

Yes. The SIS5210A holds 600 seven-inch or 300 thirteen-inch reels, and the two sizes can be mixed freely across its five layers. The standard drawing shows four 265 mm layers for 7-inch reels and a 415 mm bottom layer for 13-inch reels; we set the layer pitch to your mix.

How is it different from the SIS7200A?

The SIS7200A is a fixed seven-layer rack for 1,400 seven-inch reels in the warehouse. The SIS5210A is a smaller five-layer rack on casters for mixed sizes, with a battery inside, so it can carry a kit to the line. Both run the same software and API.

Does it need cables at the line?

No. The rack has a battery inside and a Wi-Fi controller on top, and the parameter table lists wired and wireless networking. Ask us for battery capacity and charging time for your shift pattern.

How does our MES talk to the rack?

Over HTTP with JSON, the same API as our fixed racks: your system sends light-on, light-off and standby commands with a slot index and colour, and the rack reports put-away and pick events back. Token authentication is optional.

Does it work without an ERP or MES?

Yes. The web-based warehouse software runs on its own with BOM and work-order import from Excel, CSV or XML, and links to SAP, Oracle, HopFlow or your MES when you are ready.

What do installation and support look like?

Free installation and training, worldwide remote support, spare parts with a 1-day lead time and about one month for a customised solution, as stated in our proposal documents.

Is the 6-month payback guaranteed?

No. It is the figure printed on the product sheet. We have no SIS5210A customer case to show yet, so we work out payback with your own labour figures in the worksheet on this page.
