The cut removed 1.38 to 1.61 mm of lead

Reading over the component: 25.00 mm before, then 23.62 mm and 23.39 mm after. That is 1.38 mm and 1.61 mm of lead taken off two joints on the same board.

The protruding lead fell by 60–71%

2.28 mm of lead stood below the board before cutting. After cutting it was 0.90 mm on one sample and 0.67 mm on the other — a reduction of 60.5% and 70.6%.

Both results sit inside the rated window

The SF320AT is specified for a finished lead length of 0.5–6 mm. At 0.67 mm and 0.90 mm, both measured joints fall inside that range rather than at its edge.

02 — The tolerance question

Why the two samples differ, and what the original report says about it

The two after-cut samples do not read identically — 0.90 mm against 0.67 mm. That is a real spread, and the original report names its cause rather than leaving it unexplained.

The report attributes the spread to PCB warp. Its own note reads: “PCB Warp down would caused the tolerance”. A board that is not flat presents a different height to the cutter at different points, so the finished lead length varies across the panel.

That makes the spread a property of the board rather than a fault in the cutter. If your assembly needs a tighter window than 0.67–0.90 mm, the first place to look is board flatness and how the panel is supported during cutting — a question about your board, and one worth putting to an application engineer before you choose a machine.

07 — FAQ

Questions this measurement raises

The six questions that come up most often about this result. The answers are the same ones our application engineers give.

What lead length can the SF320AT cut to?
The machine is specified for a finished lead length of 0.5–6 mm. On the board shown on this page it measured 0.90 mm and 0.67 mm on two joints of the same panel.
What is the largest PCB the SF320AT accepts?
320 × 250 mm, with a minimum board edge of 3 mm. The smallest board it will take is 20 × 50 mm.
Can it cut leads on boards with tall components fitted?
Yes. The specification allows a maximum component height of 110 mm above the board, and the cutting axes cover X 400 mm, Y 280 mm and Z 100 mm with 360° rotation.
Why did the two samples differ, 0.90 mm against 0.67 mm?
The demo report attributes the spread to PCB warp; its own note reads “PCB Warp down would caused the tolerance”. A board that is not flat presents a different height to the cutter at different points, so the finished lead length varies across the panel. It is a property of the board rather than a fault in the cutter.
How are the cut leads removed from the board?
By vacuum aspiration. The cutter head trims the lead and the offcut is drawn away, so no loose lead scrap is left on the board or inside the machine.
Does the SF320AT run inline or stand alone?
It runs inline. Boards enter from the upstream machine, are cut, and leave to the next stage on the line conveyor, with no operator needed at the cutter. Board flow can be left-to-right or right-to-left.
